Privacy policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026 (German: “Stand: 14.11.2026”)

This policy covers the app Next Stop (Android and Wear OS) and this website. In short: Next Stop has no account, no own server, no ads, no tracking, no analytics and no crash reports. I do not receive any data from you. What the app sends to other services is listed below. The German version is the primary one.

Next Stop is a private open-source project (GPL-3.0) and not an offering of BVG, VBB or Deutsche Bahn. You can check the source code on GitHub.

2. What does not happen

  • No account, no sign-in, no registration.
  • No own server that receives data from you.
  • No analytics, no ads, no advertising ID, no tracking, no crash reporting.
  • Settings, favourites, saved places and routes and recent destinations stay on your device.

3. Which services receive requests

To show timetables, maps and search, your device (phone or watch) sends requests directly to the following public services. Every request technically carries your IP address; the app also sends an identifier like NextStop/version with the app version, but no device identifier. All connections use HTTPS.

These services are independent third parties with their own terms and privacy policies; I have no influence on how long they keep requests. The data itself (such as maps) comes from OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

Own server: In the settings you can enter your own server address in place of the public BVG REST service. Those requests (also in the background and in widgets) then go to the server you chose. You are responsible for that server.

The OpenStreetMap station data for Berlin and further German states is part of the app or is read from the device; the app does not download it from a server. The line network for the city map is built by the app itself from real-time queries to the timetable services.

4. Location

The app uses your location (precise or approximate; you can choose approximate) only while it is open: for nearby stops, the map, “from here” and walking routes. The coordinates go into the requests to the services in section 3. Without location permission the app keeps working and starts at Alexanderplatz. There is no background location access.

  • Widgets refresh departures with the last position the app recorded while you used it (kept on the device only); the app does not ask for location in the background. That position is sent to the timetable services.
  • Disruption watch (optional, switched on by you): a background job (WorkManager) checks, at an interval or at times you choose, saved routes with the bell on and, if you switch it on, saved places for disruptions. It uses only the saved stops and coordinates of those routes and places, not your current location, and sends them to the timetable services. If nothing is switched on, nothing runs. Notices already shown are remembered on the device for a few days.
  • Live trip: the foreground service refreshes the trip you started (departure times, delays) and shows it in an ongoing notification. It uses no location, microphone or camera and ends five minutes after arrival or when you end the trip.

5. Contacts and calendar (optional)

Both permissions are optional; the app works fully without them. Contacts: when you open “contacts” in the search, the app reads the postal addresses of your contacts while the list is open; nothing is stored or logged. If you tap a contact, its address is sent like a typed search text to Photon and the timetable services. If allowed, contacts with an address can also be copied to the paired watch (device to device, no third party). Calendar: the app reads the next appointments with a location on demand (7 days, at most 4) and does not store them; only the location of the appointment you pick is searched as a destination. “Add to calendar” only hands an event to your calendar app.

6. Notifications

The app posts notifications locally on your device (live trip, get-off reminder, disruptions). There is no push service and no server that sends notifications. The permission is optional; on Android 16 and later the trip can appear as a “Live Update”.

7. Android backup

Android can include settings, favourites, saved places and routes and recent destinations in the Google backup of your account or transfer them to a new device. You control this in the system settings; the cloud backup is only used where it can be encrypted on the device (a screen lock is set). Access keys for data services, caches and widget state are excluded. I have no access to this backup; Google’s terms apply.

8. Live link and “route” link

You can share a trip or a connection as a link. The trip sits in the part of the link after the “#” (fragment), which browsers never send to a server. The recipient’s page (live page, route page) queries the public timetable services (BVG REST, Transitous) and OpenFreeMap from the recipient’s own browser, so their IP address and request go to those services. The map library MapLibre is served by this website itself; there is no request to a third-party CDN. The start of your trip is not in a live link (the trip there begins at the first stop); when it leads to a saved place such as “Home”, the page shows only its name and the area to about 100 m, not the address. The pages show the planned trip with real-time data, not the GPS position of your phone. The name a live link shows is set by you and is optional. The address of the live page can be changed in the settings.

9. Watch app (Wear OS)

Phone and watch exchange trips, favourites and, if used, contact addresses directly between the devices through the Wearable Data Layer of Google Play services. The watch app can also send requests on its own to the services in section 3 and uses its own location for that, only while it is in use.

10. This website

The website is static, sets no cookies, contains no tracking and loads nothing from third parties on this page. It is served by GitHub Pages (provider: GitHub, Inc.). As the host, GitHub logs requests for technical reasons (such as IP address, time, page requested, browser identifier). I have no access to these logs and evaluate nothing.

11. Legal bases

  • Requests to timetable, routing, search and map services (sections 3, 4, 8): made to deliver the function you call up in the app, i.e. to provide the use of the app (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), and in the legitimate interest in a working app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • Location, contacts, calendar, notifications: your consent through the system permission (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR; for access to the device also section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). You can withdraw it at any time in the Android settings; the app then works without it.
  • Website logs at the host: legitimate interest in operation and security (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

12. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection, and the right to withdraw consent. I store no data about you: there is no account and no server, so all I can do is confirm that nothing is held. You delete local data by clearing the app’s storage in the Android settings or by uninstalling it. For data held by the services in section 3, contact the respective provider.
